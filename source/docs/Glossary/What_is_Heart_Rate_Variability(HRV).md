## What is Heart Rate Variability (HRV)?

Heart Rate Variability (HRV) is a measure of the variation in the time between each heartbeat. Instead of your heart beating at a constant pace, the time between beats naturally varies. HRV captures these changes and helps provide insights into your body’s overall health and resilience.

A **higher HRV** means your heart rate is more variable, which is a good sign that your body can easily adapt and recover. A **lower HRV** indicates less variation between heartbeats, which may be associated with stress, anxiety, or other health conditions.

### How HRV Relates to Meditation and Your Body

HRV is linked to your **Autonomic Nervous System (ANS)**, which controls automatic bodily functions like breathing and heart rate. When you’re relaxed and calm, your HRV tends to be higher, showing that your body is in a good state for recovery. On the other hand, stress or anxiety can lower HRV, indicating that your body is less adaptable in handling pressure.

Meditation has been shown to positively influence HRV by promoting relaxation and reducing stress. With regular practice, meditation can help increase your HRV, making you more resilient to daily stressors.

### Factors that Influence HRV

1. **Stress:**  
   High stress can lower your HRV, making it harder for your body to adapt and recover.

2. **Relaxation:**  
   As your body relaxes, your HRV increases, reflecting a greater ability to recover and adapt.

3. **Overall Health:**  
   Illness, fatigue, and emotional challenges like anxiety can reduce your HRV, while good health and a balanced lifestyle tend to increase it.

### How Meditation Affects HRV

By practicing meditation regularly, you can help boost your HRV over time. Meditation helps manage stress and puts your body into a more balanced, relaxed state, which naturally increases HRV. As you continue to meditate, you’ll likely see gradual improvements in your HRV, indicating that your body is becoming more resilient to stress.
