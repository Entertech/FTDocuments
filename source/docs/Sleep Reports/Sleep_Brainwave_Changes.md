## Understanding Sleep Brainwaves with the Brain Tag

Your Flowtime Brain Tag goes beyond just tracking sleep duration and position—it also monitors the brainwaves that flow through your mind during the night. Brainwaves are electrical patterns generated by the brain, and they change as you move through different stages of sleep. The Brain Tag provides you with a detailed graph showing how five key brainwave rhythms fluctuate throughout the night.

#### The Five Brainwave Rhythms
Here’s a breakdown of the five main brainwave rhythms that the Brain Tag tracks:

1. **Delta Waves (0.5-4 Hz):**  
   Delta waves are the slowest brainwaves and are most prominent during deep, restorative sleep (also known as slow-wave sleep). This is the stage where your body heals and regenerates tissues, builds bone and muscle, and strengthens your immune system.

2. **Theta Waves (4-8 Hz):**  
   Theta waves are associated with lighter stages of sleep, particularly in the early phases of sleep onset and during REM sleep (the dream stage). These waves are linked to creativity, deep relaxation, and emotional processing.

3. **Alpha Waves (8-12 Hz):**  
   Alpha waves are commonly present during the transition between wakefulness and sleep. These waves are often associated with a relaxed but awake state and can appear during brief awakenings or moments of drowsiness.

4. **Beta Waves (12-30 Hz):**  
   Beta waves typically occur when you’re awake and alert, but they can also appear during moments of heightened mental activity, even while asleep. Spikes in beta activity during sleep may reflect stress or restlessness.

5. **Gamma Waves (30-100 Hz):**  
   Gamma waves are the fastest of the brainwaves and are associated with high-level information processing and cognitive functioning. During sleep, gamma activity may play a role in learning and memory consolidation.

#### How the Graph Helps You Understand Your Sleep
The Brain Tag visualizes these five brainwave rhythms on a graph, showing how they change throughout the night as you move through different sleep stages. This graph gives you a clear picture of your sleep architecture, helping you understand how much time you spend in light sleep, deep sleep, and REM sleep.

#### Why Monitoring Brainwaves Matters
By tracking your brainwave rhythms, the Brain Tag helps you get a deeper understanding of your sleep quality. For example:
- A consistent pattern of delta waves indicates restorative deep sleep.
- Spikes in beta waves could signal stress or interruptions in your sleep cycle.
- Prolonged periods of theta and gamma waves during REM sleep suggest vivid dreaming and emotional processing.

With this data, you can gain insights into how well your brain is functioning during sleep and how different factors (like stress, diet, or environment) might be affecting your brain activity.
